# Using AnkiWeb to Study Languages

A practical guide to learning vocabulary and phrases with **Anki** and **AnkiWeb**, the free spaced-repetition flashcard system. Fifteen to twenty minutes a day, every day, will build vocabulary faster than occasional long cramming sessions.

## How It Works

Anki uses **spaced repetition**: it shows each card just before you are likely to forget it. Words you know well come back rarely, and difficult ones come back often.

[AnkiWeb](https://ankiweb.net/) is Anki's free online service. It does two jobs:

- **Sync** your decks and review history between computers and phones.
- **Study in a browser** on any device, with nothing installed.

> Anki is a memory tool, not a whole course. It works best alongside real input such as reading, listening, and conversation. Use it to keep the words you meet there.

## 1. Getting Started

| Platform | App | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Windows, macOS, Linux | [Anki](https://apps.ankiweb.net/) | Free |
| Android | AnkiDroid | Free |
| iPhone and iPad | AnkiMobile | Paid (funds Anki's development) |
| Any browser | [AnkiWeb](https://ankiweb.net/) | Free |

**Setup**

1. Create a free account at [ankiweb.net](https://ankiweb.net/).
2. Install Anki on your computer and phone.
3. Log in with your AnkiWeb account in each app, then click **Sync**.
4. Sync at the **start and end of every session** to avoid conflicts between devices.

> ⚠️ AnkiWeb may delete accounts that go unused for several months. Sync regularly, and keep a local backup with **File → Export** in desktop Anki.

## 2. Getting Decks

Start with one shared frequency deck for the basics, then add your own cards for words you meet in real life.

### Shared decks

Browse them at [ankiweb.net/shared/decks](https://ankiweb.net/shared/decks). Search by language, for example `Korean`, `Spanish frequency`, or `JLPT N5`.

1. Download the deck file (`.apkg`).
2. Import it in desktop Anki (**File → Import**) or AnkiDroid. The browser version of AnkiWeb cannot import decks.
3. Sync. The deck then appears on AnkiWeb and your other devices.

**A good shared deck has:**

- Words in **frequency order**, with the most common first.
- **Native-speaker audio**.
- **Example sentences**, not just single words.
- Recent updates and good ratings.

### Your own cards

Words from your textbook, shows, or conversations stick best because you already have context for them. You can add them in any app, or on AnkiWeb under **Add**.

| Source | Best for | Drawback |
|---|---|---|
| Shared deck | A fast start on core vocabulary | Cards lack personal context |
| Your own cards | Words you actually meet | Takes time to make |
| **Mix of both** | **Most learners** | Needs a daily limit on new cards |

## 3. Designing Good Language Cards

A good card tests one thing, shows the word in a sentence, and includes audio.

| Card type | Front | Back | Trains |
|---|---|---|---|
| Recognition | Target-language word | Meaning | Reading and listening |
| Production | Meaning or picture | Target-language word | Speaking and writing |
| Listening | Audio only | Word and meaning | Understanding speech |
| Cloze | Sentence with a gap | The missing word | Grammar and word use |

The built-in note type **Basic (and reversed card)** creates both recognition and production cards automatically.

**What to include on a card**

- The word, plus its gender, plural, or reading where relevant (for example Korean `먹다` with its polite form `먹어요`).
- One short example sentence with its translation.
- Native-speaker audio. Shared decks often include it; for your own cards, record yourself or use a text-to-speech add-on.
- A picture for concrete nouns, which is faster to recall than a translation.

**Example card**

```text
Front:  먹다  🔊
Back:   to eat
        저는 아침을 먹어요. — I eat breakfast.
```

Keep each card to one word or one grammar point. If you keep failing a card, split it into smaller cards or add an example.

## 4. Daily Study Routine

**Clear all due reviews every day before adding new cards.** Missed days pile up, and a growing backlog is the main reason people quit.

1. Sync.
2. Finish all **due** reviews.
3. Learn your new cards for the day.
4. Say each answer **out loud** before revealing it.
5. Sync again.

**Grading buttons**

| Button | When to press it |
|---|---|
| **Again** | You forgot it. Grade honestly so the schedule stays accurate. |
| **Hard** | You remembered it, but only with real effort. Use sparingly. |
| **Good** | You remembered it. This should be most of your answers. |
| **Easy** | It was effortless. Use sparingly. |

## 5. Recommended Settings

In desktop Anki, open these by clicking the gear icon next to a deck, then **Options**.

| Setting | Suggested value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| New cards/day | 10–20 | Each new card adds several reviews over the following weeks |
| Maximum reviews/day | 200 or more | A low cap creates a backlog |
| Scheduler | FSRS (available in recent versions) | Schedules reviews more efficiently than the older default |
| Desired retention | 0.85–0.90 | Higher values mean many more reviews for small gains |

As a rough guide, 15 new cards a day takes about 20–30 minutes once reviews build up. If sessions start running long, lower the number of new cards.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Adding too many new cards at once | Keep it to 10–20 a day |
| Skipping days | Always do at least the due reviews |
| Pressing **Good** when you only guessed | Press **Again** |
| Single-word cards with no context | Add an example sentence and audio |
| Only recognition cards | Add production cards so you can speak, not just understand |
| Many small decks | Use one deck per language and organize topics with tags |
| Using Anki as your only study method | Pair it with reading, listening, and conversation |

**More tips**

- Use AnkiWeb in a browser for short reviews on a shared or work computer, with nothing to install.
- Suspend cards you no longer need instead of deleting them (**Browse → Toggle Suspend**).
- Tag cards by source, such as `textbook-ch3` or `drama`, so you can filter them later.

## Sample Weekly Plan

| Day | Anki (about 20–30 min) | Other practice |
|---|---|---|
| Monday–Friday | Reviews + 15 new cards | 15 min reading or listening |
| Saturday | Reviews only; add 10–20 of your own cards | Watch a show or video in the language |
| Sunday | Reviews only | Conversation practice or writing |
